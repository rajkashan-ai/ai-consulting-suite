# Recommending a cadence

> Split out of `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-09-15, when that file passed its cap. The rule
> is in `CLAUDE.md` §2b. This is the reasoning and the method behind it.
>
> **Why it exists.** Raj, 15 September: the tool should recommend how often to
> post, from data and evidence, rather than ask. The instinct is right and the
> evidence is thinner than it looks, so this file is mostly about what we are
> allowed to say.

---

## What it may rest on

**Say the number, then say what it costs.** "Two a week. That is 9 posts and 9 photographs between
now and 13 October, across Instagram and Facebook." Every line of the reason is a fact we hold:

| May rest on | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| What the month costs them | Our own arithmetic: posts, photographs, channels |
| What they told us about their time | Their answer, quoted back |
| Which channels they have and which are quiet | The profile, confirmed with them |
| **How much of last month they actually posted** | **Our own tool, from month two.** The only measured evidence in the feature, and it arrives free |

**And what it may never rest on.** We cannot count what they post now. Instagram's `robots.txt`
prohibits automated collection and names `ClaudeBot` with `Disallow: /`, a logged-out profile
returns nine characters of text, and Facebook returns 400 (recorded 14 September, on the Tracker).
So there is no "you currently post twice a month" available to us, and no benchmark either: "firms
posting three times a week get more enquiries" is the easiest sentence in this feature to write and
we have no source for it.

**The step up is offered in cost and coverage, never in results.** "Three a week would let your
beard work have its own thread instead of one post a month" is ours to say. "Three a week will get
you more enquiries" is not, and no phrasing of it is. A guard refuses the second.


## What "with suggestions for improvement" means, exactly

Raj asked for capacity **plus a path up**. Both halves are needed, because a
recommendation that only ever says "do what you can manage" is efficiency-only,
and the practice's standing rule is that efficiency is table stakes and the
growth lens is where the case actually lives.

So the step up has two sentences and they follow different rules.

**What it costs.** Arithmetic we own. "Three a week is 13 photographs this month
against 9." Always safe.

**What it covers.** A gap we can point at. Three shapes, all checkable:

| Shape | Example | Why it is safe |
|---|---|---|
| A channel they own and are not using | "You have a Google Business Profile and nothing has gone on it. One extra post a week would cover it" | We can see the profile exists and is empty |
| A subject the plan cannot fit | "At two a week your beard work gets one post a month. At three it gets its own thread" | It is a fact about our own plan |
| Their own completion, from month two | "You marked 7 of 9 done last month" | Measured, by us, about them |

**What it must never say.** Anything about what happens next. More enquiries,
more bookings, more followers, more reach, better engagement, growth, any
percentage. `findPromisedResults` refuses all of it, and it refuses the polite
forms too: "should help you", "tends to", "often leads to".

The line is not about hedging. It is that we do not know, nobody has told us,
and the owner will act on it.

## Stepping down

The same machinery, and it matters more. An owner who managed 3 of 9 is not told
to try harder. They are moved to once a week and told why, in a line that does
not read as a telling-off: "You posted 3 of 9 last month. Once a week is 4, and
four you will finish."

**Never step down more than one notch at a time, and never below once a week.**
Below that the tool has stopped being worth opening.

## The first run, when there is no last month

Every reason is arithmetic and their own answer. That is thinner than month two
and it is still a recommendation, so it is made plainly rather than hedged. What
it must not do is imply a measurement it does not have: "based on your posting
history" is a lie on run one.
